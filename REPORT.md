# From 76,000 lines of Lean to a short account of the proof, with every step checked against the code

*lean-proof-xray, 26 November 2026. An experiment report. Nothing here has been reviewed by a mathematician;
that is what we are asking for.*

## The question

A machine-written Lean proof is correct by construction and unreadable by size. Can its **approach** be
recovered from the code itself, cheaply, in a way that does not ask the reader to trust the narrator?

## The method: four stages, one of them a language model

| Stage | Who | What it does | Deterministic? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Map | `xray-repo` / `xray-export` (no AI) | From Lean's own export: dependency graph, proof cone, dominator tree. Gives the **junction** of the proof and how much of the proof sits under each step. | yes |
| 2. Questions | `arac/hikaye/sorular.py` (no AI) | From the map and the junction's own statement, by fixed rules: the questions any account of this proof must answer. | yes |
| 3. Story | a language model (Claude), under [`arac/hikaye/ANLATICI.md`](arac/hikaye/ANLATICI.md) | Reads the map, asks for the code of single declarations (comments stripped), answers the required questions, writes a short account and a list of checkable claims. | no |
| 4. Check | `arac/hikaye/denetle.py` (no AI) | Tests every claim against the map (the step exists, sits where the story says, carries the share the story says), flags heavy steps the story never mentions, and refuses a story that leaves a required question unanswered. | yes |

The model never sees the whole proof. The map tells it where to look, the questions tell it what it may not
skip, and the checker tells the reader what the model got right about the structure.

## What happened, in order

**1. Map only, five projects.** Five independent narrators, given the map and statements but no code. After
revision all 310 structural claims were confirmed (first drafts contained invented abbreviations and omitted
heavy steps; the checker caught them).

| Project | Claims confirmed | Narrator's own rating | Our reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| PFR | 54 / 54 | 4 / 5 | approach recovered (the narrator recognised the theorem from training) |
| Sphere eversion | 58 / 58 | 4 / 5 | approach recovered (recognised) |
| Carleson | 87 / 87 | 4 / 5 | reduction chain recovered, not the "why" (recognised) |
| RH, ≈76k-line edition | 56 / 56 | 2.5 / 5 | skeleton and ingredients right, central idea missing |
| RH, ≈17M-line original | 55 / 55 | 2 / 5 | "an accurate table of contents" (the narrator's words) |

On the two new proofs the map alone gave a correct skeleton and no mechanism.

**2. Map plus junction code, questions asked by hand.** One narrator on the ≈76k-line edition, allowed to read
the code of single declarations. After we asked it four specific questions (where do the primes enter, what
cancels, why m lifts, where are the side branches used) it reached the mechanism in 14 lookups.

**3. The same protocol again, without our questions.** A fresh narrator who had seen nothing. It produced the
same skeleton (24 of the first story's 28 step names reappear; the reduction, the squeeze between an upper and
a lower bound, the product formula) but **not** the mechanism: nothing about the chain of lifts or the choice
m ≈ √x. So the depth of the first story came from our questions, not from the protocol.

**4. Questions generated by rule.** We replaced our hand-written questions by `sorular.py`, which derives them
from the map, and made the checker refuse a story that skips one. A third fresh narrator, given only the
protocol, answered all 10, had 43 of 43 claims confirmed, used 16 tool calls, and reached the mechanism on its
own. For this proof the generated questions were:

- **Q1.** What is Data (open its definition)? Which step of the proof produces it or bounds it, and by what mechanism? Follow the code at least two levels below that step.
- **Q2.** What is remainder (open its definition)? Which step of the proof produces it or bounds it, and by what mechanism? Follow the code at least two levels below that step.
- **Q3.** What is budget (open its definition)? Which step of the proof produces it or bounds it, and by what mechanism? Follow the code at least two levels below that step.
- **Q4.** In `eventually_primeError_le_rpow`, how is `star_inequality` turned into the conclusion? Which parameters are chosen there, how large is each relative to the main variable, and why does that choice give the bound in the statement of `eventually_primeError_le_rpow`?
- **Q5.** When the parts of `star_inequality` are combined, what cancels, and what is left?
- **Q6.** Is anything in the construction repeated a variable number of times (a chain, an iteration, a tower)? If so: what is repeated, how many times, what does each repetition contribute, and why is that number of repetitions the right one?
- **Q7.** Where is `Data.integral_forms_long` consumed (which declaration calls it), and what does it contribute to `star_inequality`?
- **Q8.** Where is `sphericalCompat_of_odd` consumed (which declaration calls it), and what does it contribute to `star_inequality`?
- **Q9.** Where is `Data.exists_family_covolumeSq_le` consumed (which declaration calls it), and what does it contribute to `star_inequality`?
- **Q10.** Where is `Data.ramifiedColumns_fill_rows` consumed (which declaration calls it), and what does it contribute to `star_inequality`?

None of them mentions this proof's mathematics; the same rules produce the questions for any proof.

## The result

The third narrator's account of the ≈76k-line proof, verbatim ([full story with all answers](sinama/grh76/HIKAYE.md)):

1. The top level (`New.grh`, feeding `riemannHypothesis`) reduces everything to a one-sided estimate: for a suitable prime d in a fixed residue class, a weighted prime-counting error called primeError must eventually be at most x^(8ε − 1/2), and a Landau-type argument on its Laplace transform (whose poles count zeros of L-functions minus zeros of their quadratic twists by d) then forbids zeros to the right of 1/2 + 8ε.
2. For each large x the proof builds a package of auxiliary choices called Data (an integer m ≈ √x that is a power of 2, an integer r ≈ x^5 that is a power of 3, a huge scale b with log b = x^ε, two auxiliary primes between b^10 and b^11, a three-term-free set of place classes called thick), and rewrites primeError(x) as m·remainder divided by m·a·#thick, plus a term at a second scale x₁ that is made negligible by picking x₁ in a window.
3. The core, `star_inequality`, says m·remainder ≤ (log b)^2·budget, and it is proved by measuring one complex vector space of automorphic forms (called rows) in two ways: `Data.exists_family_covolumeSq_le` produces almost a full basis of forms that are integral up to a small index and whose Gram determinant is at most exp(n·(mainHeight − m·remainder + log b·budget)), while `Data.integral_forms_long` shows that any such nearly-integral nearly-full family must have log-determinant at least rowDim·(mainHeight − 3·log b·budget).
4. When the two bounds are compared the large quantity mainHeight (= m·k·dirCost) cancels exactly, and what is left is m·remainder ≤ 4·log b·budget + small terms; since budget does not grow with m, dividing by m ≈ √x is what gives the square-root saving.
5. The lower bound is a product-formula ("height") argument: the volume of rows factors into m lift volumes, one per step of an m-fold chain of lifts, and each factor is compared between the trivial twist and a family of auxiliary twists, with the p-adic denominators controlled by Galois representations attached to the forms (`sphericalCompat_of_odd`, `Model.exists_galois_rep`) through a Selmer-length/Ribet-style bound (`exists_isFitFamily`, `denominatorLe_rowBudget`, `ribet_columns`).

And for a general reader:

> The proof turns a question about zeros into a question about how evenly primes are spread in certain patterns. It then builds, for each large number x, a big finite-dimensional space of highly symmetric functions. It measures the size of that space in two independent ways, once from above using explicitly constructed small functions and once from below using the fact that whole-number objects cannot be too small. The two measurements share an enormous common term that drops out, and what remains is exactly the prime-counting error, multiplied by about the square root of x. Because the leftover on the other side is small, the error must be small, which is the square-root accuracy the hypothesis demands. The lower measurement relies on deep arithmetic symmetries (Galois representations) to control denominators.

We compared this with the edition's own guide and with the author's four-page note, neither of which any
narrator saw. The reduction to a one-sided bound, the main inequality and the theorems it joins, the squeeze
between an upper and a lower bound for the same covolume, the product formula, the chain of m lifts and the
choice of m near √x all agree with them. We are not competent to judge more than that agreement.

## How stable is it?

Three independent narrators wrote about the same proof. The wording differs every time. The skeleton did not:
all three give the same reduction and the same squeeze, and all pass the checker. The mechanism appeared in two
of three: in the one we questioned by hand and in the one questioned by rule, not in the one left to itself.
That is three runs of one model on one proof. It is evidence, not a guarantee.

## What is checked and what is not

- **Checked, mechanically:** that the story talks about real steps of this proof, in their real places, with
  their real weights; that it leaves out no heavy step; that it answers every required question.
- **Not checked:** the mathematical reading ("the volume factors into m lift volumes", "the product is 1, so the
  heights sum to at least 0"). That is the model's reading of code. It needs a mathematician.

## What the narrator could not tell

- Why the Gram determinant of rows is genuinely tied to the Chowla–Selberg-type constant and to dirCost: I read the definitions (jetSize, primeSum, mainAsk) and the Stirling step in the per-row height bound, but not the geometric source of the factorial sizes.
- What the objects rows, Column, Bμ, goodLift and the spaces Y, W concretely are (which groups, which weights); I only saw them used.
- How `Attached.local_global_of_eigencharacter` itself is used inside `star_inequality`; for `sphericalCompat_of_odd` I traced the route through the lemmas sphericalCompat_std, attached_galRep and exists_hasRow_auxPair into `denominatorLe_rowBudget` and `exists_isFitFamily`, but not what the ramified local-global statement is needed for.
- How `exchange_theorem`, `Data.exists_besselSystem`, `exists_isWeakBaseChange_of_matching` (beyond its use inside `sphericalCompat_of_odd`), `Data.exists_isTriangle` and `Data.hasGap_gridParam` enter `star_inequality`; I read only the statement of `exchange_theorem`.
- That the number f of infinite places is comparable to towerDeg, needed for my arithmetic f/#thick ≤ log b in Q4 (guess); the final inequality there is closed by an automated magnitude tactic I did not unfold.
- Why the thick set must be three-term-free, and the role of r ≈ x^5 beyond k = r + 1 − m and h₂ = r^8.
- The interior of `Model.exists_galois_rep`, `ellAdicCohom.woods_hole`, `nonempty_universalFamily`, `linearIndepOn_hodgeTateMap_pointClass`, `Filling.energy_le_of_linkEnergy_le`, `Filling.energy_le_of_level_pairs`, `Filling.sum_le_of_positive_type`, `Filling.energy_fixingSet_le`, `exists_comparison_forms` (statement only), and the mark-operator chain `Data.IsMarked.hasType_prod_markOperator_pow`, `Data.IsMarked.hasType_markOperator`, `Columns.hasType_markOperator`, `TwistingData.hasType_twisted_hecke`.
- Whether residueClass is the von Mangoldt function restricted to a class (guess from the L-series lemma names).

## Limits

- One proof, one model (Claude), three runs. The clean edition has meaningful names; the 17-million-line
  original does not, and map-plus-code has not been tried on it yet.
- The narrators recognised the three older projects from training, so those results say little.
- Comments were stripped so that the story comes from code, not from what the code says about itself. A model
  may still be helped by identifier names.
- The checker cannot tell a correct mathematical explanation from a plausible one.
- The question rules were written after seeing which questions had worked on this proof. Whether they are the
  right questions for other proofs is untested.
